2.4 GHz Phased Array Project

This is a report that my team and I made for our final project for 6.2300, a class on electromagnetic waves and applications at MIT. The class covers the physics behind electromagnetic waves, how they propagate, and how they are used in different technologies, such as antennas and waveguides. For our final project, we designed a 2.4 GHz phased array using the course-provided VNAs as our signal source, Wilkinson power dividers as our signal splitters, and patch antennas as our radiating elements. We designed and simulated the Wilkinson power dividers in HFSS to ensure optimal power transfer at the desired frequency. I designed the patch antennas in KiCad, and milled out all of the deisgned boards using circuit mills on-campus. To achieve a phase shift and test the beam steering capabilities of our phased array, we created a fixed delay in some boards by extending the lengths of certain traces by a specific amount to a 90 degree phase shift between antennas, shifting the center of the beam by 30 degrees.
