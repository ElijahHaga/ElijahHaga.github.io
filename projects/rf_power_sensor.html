High Frequency RF Power Sensor Project

This is a report that my team and I made for our final project for 6.2600, a class on micro- and nano-fabrication at MIT. The class covers the physics and processes behind micro- and nano-fabrication, including photolithography, etching, deposition, and doping. For our final project, I designed a high frequency (~65-100 GHz) RF power sensor using a Schottky diode coupled with an antenna. The Schottky diode rectifies any incident RF signal, and the output DC power should scale linearly with the input RF power. I designed the whole device using phidl and Python, testing the resulting output using KLayout. We then fabricated the device in the on-campus cleanroom, as described in the report. The wafer features three distinct antenna designs (a dual dipole, a folded dipole, and a resistively-tapered dipole) and sweeps different diode dimensions to test the effects of different parameters on the performance of the device.
