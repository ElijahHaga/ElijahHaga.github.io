LED Ring Circuit Board

This circuit board is an LED ring PCB that I designed in Flux, a free browser-based ECAD. It uses an STM32F030CCT6 microcontroller and a BMI160 6-axis gyroscope to control 20 LEDs depending on the orientation of the circuit board. It is charged and programmed by a USB-C port. I thought of, researched, and designed this circuit board completely on my own, doing my own parts selection and BOM while learning how to use Flux. I prefer Altium Designer, but I used Flux to see how well I could adapt my skills to a completely different ECAD.

Lithium-Ion Battery

For my final project in AP Chem, I chose to research and fabricate a lithium-ion battery using the materials in my high school's lab to get a better understanding of how Lithium-ion batteries work. I only had a month to complete this assignment, so I could not document much of the process, but I have attached my lab report about my findings. As my report states, I used a Lithium Cobalt Oxide (LiCoO2) cathode (specifically low-temperature LiCoO2, I explain this important distinction in my paper) and a graphite anode. These materials were ground into powders to achieve an even charge distribution, then mixed with a binding agent (in my case, PTFE, commonly known as Teflon), a conductive agent (I used carbon black), and a solvent to dissolve the binder, which I used isopropanol (C3H8O) for. The mixture was distributed evenly to an aluminum sheet for the cathode and a copper sheet for the anode. An ion bridge must be constructed to allow the lithium ions to travel between the cathode and anode, which I made out of polyethylene ((C2H4)n) due to its high availability (it's what plastic bags are made of) soaked in lithium perchlorate (LiClO4) as an electrolyte dissolved in ethyl-methyl-carbonate (C4H8O3), which I had to harvest from another battery as they were impossible to create with the materials provided to me. The ion bridge was sandwiched between the anode and cathode, and then a wire with a resistor and LED was connected to the anode and cathode, along with an oscilloscope probe. I was successfully able to measure a voltage of around 4 Volts for a short period before the battery caught on fire, likely due to the anode and cathode powders spilling over the separator and shorting together. Although the battery had to be destroyed, it proved successful in discharging for a short period.
